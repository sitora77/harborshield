# HarborShield v0.2 · joint-risk experiment report

Protocol date: 4 October 2026. Generated by `scripts/run_experiments.py`.

## Scope and protocol

All cost/risk results below come from an **uncalibrated synthetic model**. The official MPA arrivals dataset is not an input to these experiments.

20 containers; USD250,000 electronics cargo **per container**; coverage 90%; deductible 1%; delay cost USD500/container/day; route-share cap 70%; mean-transit limit 30 days; risk weight 0.5; latent shared-shock strength 0.35. Training: 1,000 worlds, seed 11; independent evaluation: 10,000 worlds, seed 20261004; stability seeds 11,22,33,44,55.

The 30-day report constraint is fixed across all four regimes. The dashboard defaults to 22 days and can correctly report infeasibility under severe disruption. A transit constraint on the **expected** duration is not a delivery-time guarantee.

Allocations below are in the order **Direct / Busan / Klang+road**. CVaR95 is the average cost in the worst 5% of the batch-cost distribution, with exact fractional boundary mass.

Do not report an improvement when the interval spans zero. Identical policies produce an exactly zero paired difference. The optimisation objective is mean + 0.5 × (CVaR95 − mean); it is not a promise to minimise held-out CVaR alone.

## Interpretation and limitations

- The joint-risk method evaluates every feasible integer allocation on training data. Its optimum is exact **for those samples and constraints**, not for unknown real shipping outcomes.
- Cost-only uses the same training worlds and feasibility constraints. Balanced feasible minimises squared deviation from equal thirds; additive-tail proxy sums standalone per-container tail penalties.
- Common random numbers make test-policy comparisons paired, not independent across policies.
- Some baseline policies can coincide, or win on held-out samples. This is a finding, not a software error.
- The route assumptions often make Busan attractive on both mean and tail cost. This limits the richness of the trade-off; do not alter assumptions solely to manufacture a winning result.
- A Gaussian dependence assumption, triangular severity, constant insurance loading, homogeneous containers and one sailing per route are simplifications; calibration, distribution shift and policy-specific insurance terms remain future work.
